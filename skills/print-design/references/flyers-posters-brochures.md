# Flyers, posters, brochures — designing for distance and sequence

These are read in motion: walked past, picked up, unfolded. A card has one reading distance;
a poster has three; a brochure has an order. Design the distances and the order first, then the
surface.

## The three-distance rule (posters, flyers on a wall)

| Distance | What must work | Share of the surface |
|---|---|---|
| **Far** — 10–20 ft, walking past | **One** element: a word, an image, a colour field. Legible without stopping. | 60–80% |
| **Middle** — 3 ft, stopped | The second thing: what, when, where. Two or three lines. | 15–30% |
| **Near** — 1 ft, leaning in | Everything else: details, credits, URL, QR. | 5–10% |

The centroid poster gives all three tiers equal weight and so fails the far read entirely: it
is a flyer stuck to a wall. Decide what the far read *is* — and it is one thing — and let it own
the sheet.

**Cap height for the far read [D]:** ~1 in per 10 ft of viewing distance; a title read from
20 ft wants 2 in caps — which is roughly **200–220 pt**, because a capital is only ~0.7 of the
point size (the em). Type the size as ~1.4 × the cap height you want; the sheet size does not
change that arithmetic. Print a proof and stand where the reader will.

## Flyers (handed out, arm's length)

Two distances, not three: the glance as it is handed over (one element), and the read once
held (the rest). Sizes: half-letter, A5, DL, 4 × 6, 5 × 7.

- **One image or one headline**, not both fighting. If the image is the far read, the headline
  is the middle read, and vice versa.
- **Body copy** exists — but it is set like a page, in a text face at 9–11 pt with a real
  measure, not centred under a headline.
- **Tear-off tabs** are a *design* element when the perforation line and the tabs are drawn
  with intent (a row of identical tabs reads as a lost-cat poster; a single tab or a
  detachable card reads as considered). Perforation is a finishing spec: say so on the sheet.
- **QR codes**: ≥ 0.75 in, quiet zone of four modules, dark on light, and a caption saying
  what it does. Or none.
- **Two-sided**: the back is free. A flyer that is blank on the back gave away half its
  surface.

## Posters

- **Format is a decision.** 18 × 24 and 24 × 36 are defaults; 11 × 17 is cheap and everywhere;
  A2/A1 read as "designed"; a long vertical (12 × 36) or a square is a format break.
- **The far read owns it.** A single word at 3 in caps, one photograph full-bleed, or a colour
  field with one small object in it. Everything else is subordinate and small.
- **Type at distance** wants a face with even stroke weight and open counters — a grotesk, a
  slab, a geometric — not a hairline serif or a condensed with tight apertures, which fill in
  visually at 20 ft.
- **Grids still apply.** A poster with a strict grid and one element that violates it is the
  classic; a poster with no grid is a collage.
- **Stock**: 100 lb gloss text is the default and looks like advertising; **uncoated** or matte
  looks like art and takes a spot colour beautifully. A poster is also the one place a
  **Risograph** (two spot colours, deliberate misregistration, visible grain) is the right
  answer — and Riso is a real print method with real limits (A3 max, 1–2 colours per pass,
  no large solid fills), not a Photoshop filter.
- **Bleed for large format** is 0.25 in **[N]**.

## Brochures and folded pieces — choose the fold for the reading order

The fold is the structure. Pick it for *how the information should be disclosed*, never for
how many panels you need to fill.

| Fold | Panels | Reading order | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Half** | 4 | cover → inside spread → back | one big idea inside; a menu; an invitation |
| **Tri (roll)** | 6 | cover → inside flap (first thing seen on opening) → three-panel interior → back | the classic; sequence matters |
| **Z (accordion, 3)** | 6 | reads either as a sequence of panels or opens flat to one spread | timelines, maps, a single wide image |
| **Gate** | 6 (two flaps meet in the middle) | cover → reveal (both flaps open at once) → spread | a reveal; a product hero |
| **Accordion (4+)** | 8+ | a strip | a timeline; a set of steps; a mini-zine |
| **Roll (4-panel)** | 8 | outer → progressively inner | long sequential content |

**Physical facts [C]:**
- The panel that folds **inside** is narrower, or the piece will not close flat. On a letter
  roll (tri) fold exactly one panel tucks in: **3.6875 / 3.6875 / 3.625 in** (= 11), the inner
  panel 1/16 in short — 1/8 in on cover weights. On a 4-panel roll each successive inner panel
  steps down again. A Z-fold has no inner panel and three equal thirds. The printer's template
  has their exact numbers **[N]** — use it, and never lay out a fold as equal columns.
- **Score** any fold on stock heavier than ~100 lb text, or the fold cracks and shows white.
- **Cross-panel art**: an image that spans two panels needs those panels to be adjacent *when
  open* — draw the flat layout and mark which panel is which before placing anything.
- **The outside is a cover, not a page.** The front panel is a poster at arm's length: one
  element. The back panel is where the contact information goes.

## Menus, invitations, programmes

Same rules; the reading distance is 1 ft and the sequence is the fold. A menu is a typographic
document first — a text face with real numerals, a clear price alignment (tabular figures,
or leaders, or right-aligned — one system), section heads that are a single size step up.
An invitation is a card: stock and finish first, one hero move, restraint everywhere else.

## Slop to recoil from

- The **poster that is a big flyer** — three equal tiers, no far read.
- **Centred everything** — a poster's grid is its skeleton; centring removes it.
- **A photograph with text over it** where neither was designed for the other; add a colour
  field or a band, or choose one.
- **Six typefaces** because there are six sections.
- **A tri-fold laid out as three equal columns** with no thought for the flap that is seen
  first, or for the panel that is 1/16 in narrower.
- **QR code stuck in a corner**; **tear-tabs as an afterthought**; **body copy on a poster**.
