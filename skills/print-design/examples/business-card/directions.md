# Directions — Ash & Ember Letterpress, business card

The artifact the protocol produces before any markup. Kept beside the design so the next session
extends the concept instead of re-deriving a centroid.

## DNA — from the studio's own world, not a gallery

| Ask | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Materials** | 600 gsm cotton stock; metal and photopolymer plates; oil-based ink; a Vandercook proof press; the *impression* — type pressed into paper you can feel |
| **Motifs** | the plate's edge; the deep-relief letterform; the ember (the name); registration marks; the furniture and quoins of a locked-up forme |
| **Palette from source** | warm black ink (Pantone Black 6), cream cotton (Lettra Pearl White), one occasional flame red (Pantone 1795) they use on posters |
| **Tempo** | slow, deliberate, one sheet at a time; nothing is undone |
| **Vernacular** | "edition", "impression", "forme", "proof", "kiss" (a light impression) vs "bite" (a deep one) |
| **Rules of the world** | one colour per pass; no gradients; no hairlines below the plate's tolerance; the paper is part of the print |

## Directions — structurally different, one operator each

1. **Stock as the idea + blind deboss** (material). No ink on the front at all: a single large
   italic ampersand — the join of *Ash* and *Ember*, the name's whole meaning in one glyph —
   pressed blind into the cotton, read by tilting the card; the back carries the name in one
   warm black. *Swap test:* an ampersand is a letterpress commonplace, so this is the direction
   most at risk of designing the category — it survives only because this studio's name *is*
   the ampersand. A studio called "Redwood Press" could not use it. **Survives, narrowly.**
   (A first draft used an "ember" icon — a disc with a flame cut out. At proof size it read as
   an app icon. Discarded: an illustrative mark at 0.6 in has to be very good, and this was not.)
2. **The forme** (cross-domain grammar). The card is laid out like a locked-up forme seen from
   above: the type block, the furniture as thin rules, quoins as two small wedges, a registration
   mark. Contact lines set inside the "type area". *Swap test:* the grammar belongs to letterpress
   specifically — but a *different* letterpress studio could use it unchanged. **Fails** (it
   designs the category "letterpress studio", not this one).
3. **The impression, extreme** (extreme parameter). The name at 40 pt, deep bite, filling the
   front edge to edge, cropped by the trim so only "Ash &" is whole. *Swap test:* survives on
   form, but the crop makes the name illegible on a card meant to be handed to strangers.
   **Discarded for function.**
4. **Kiss / bite** (antagonistic pairing). Front printed with a *kiss* impression (barely there);
   back printed with a *bite* (deep). Same ink, two pressures — the card teaches the vocabulary by
   touch. *Swap test:* survives; it is a letterpress-only idea. **Runner-up.**

## Committed

**Direction 1**, with one graft from 4: the back is printed with a deliberately deep bite so the
two sides feel different in the hand. Front: the ampersand only, blind (no ink) — in the flat
proof it is shown as ink so the form can be checked, and the spec sheet says "blind deboss".
Back: name (with the same italic ampersand, small, so the two sides rhyme), role, three contact
lines, one warm black.

Everything else quiet: one typeface, one weight change, one rule, the cream of the stock.
