# Directions — Night Market, 18 × 24 in poster

Written before any markup. The subject is a fictional weekly street night market; the brief is
a poster read from across a street, then from the pavement, then from arm's length.

## DNA — from a night market's own world, not a poster gallery

| Ask | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Materials** | paper lanterns; hand-painted price boards on scrap ply; steam off woks and kettles; neon tube signs; plastic stools; cash, coins and a biscuit tin; the tarpaulin roof |
| **Motifs** | the price board — one big word, a column of items, prices in a different hand; the grid of stalls; the plume of steam; the lantern's warm disc |
| **Palette from source** | lantern orange-red at night; the cold blue of a fluorescent tube; kraft and ply; none of it clean or coated |
| **Tempo** | loud, crowded, improvised, weekly; things are lettered by hand the same afternoon |
| **Vernacular** | "stall", "board", "cash only", "while it lasts", "from 7 till late" |
| **Rules of the world** | everything is made cheap and fast and looks it, on purpose; nothing is centred by a designer; prices dominate |

## Directions — structurally different, one operator each

1. **The price board** (cross-domain grammar). The poster *is* a stall's board: one huge word,
   then a column of "items" — the stalls — with their opening times set where the prices go,
   dotted leaders between. Far read: the word. Near read: the board. *Swap test:* the board
   grammar is the market's own; a gallery opening or a gig could not use it unchanged.
   **Survives.**
2. **The colour field** (extreme parameter). Ninety percent of the sheet one lantern orange,
   one 14 pt line at the foot. *Swap test:* any red poster is any event. **Fails.**
3. **Risograph, two inks** (material). Fluorescent orange and blue, deliberately misregistered,
   steam as a halftone plume, on uncoated stock. *Swap test:* the *look* alone is the "indie
   event poster" category; it survives only when it carries a grammar that is the market's.
   **Survives as a material, not as a direction on its own.**
4. **The word, rotated** (type as the whole design). "NIGHT MARKET" in condensed caps at 6 in,
   rotated 90°, the information in the leftover strip. *Swap test:* survives, strong far read;
   but it says "a poster about typography" more than "a market". **Runner-up.**

## Committed

**Direction 1, printed as direction 3.** The price board, in two Riso inks on uncoated stock:
the far read is "NIGHT / MARKET" stacked in condensed caps at ~3.7 in cap height (≈ 380 pt — a
cap is ~0.7 of the em); the middle read is the date, time and street at 60 pt; the near read is
the board — twelve stalls with their hours as "prices", 16 pt, dotted leaders. A halftone plume
of steam in blue rises behind the word. The blue layer is offset 0.06 in from the orange, as a
real two-pass Riso would be. A perforated strip at the foot repeats the essentials, to tear off
and keep. Nothing is centred except the word.

One graft from 4: the word is set at the largest size the sheet allows, edge to edge.

## What the proof cannot show

Riso fluorescent orange is a spot ink far outside the CMYK gamut; the lint will warn on the
approximation used on screen, and that warning is correct. The spec sheet names the inks.
