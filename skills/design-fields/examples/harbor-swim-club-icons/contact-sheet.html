Above, below, or half under

Eight things from the harbour, each drawn with the same waterline. What an icon means is where its object sits in relation to the line: the flag and the thermos stand above it, the float sits half under, the tide rises through it, the harbour steps come down to meet it. One rule, eight times.

At 24 — without labels first

At 48, on cream and on navy

The rule, and the grid

24-pixel grid, 20-pixel live area, 2-pixel stroke with round caps and joins, currentColor. Every coordinate sits on the pixel grid, so the stroke renders crisp at 24 and, doubled, at 48.

The waterline is at y = 14 in every icon and is never omitted. It is the identity's one variable at icon scale: the mark's ring is the float, half under.

Not delivered at 16. There the stroke would be 1.3 pixels and the line would merge with the objects; below 20 pixels the mark's favicon is used instead. Sizes are redrawn, not scaled — and here, at that size, not drawn.
