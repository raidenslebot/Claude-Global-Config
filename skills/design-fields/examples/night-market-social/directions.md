# Directions — Night Market, the feed series

Written before any markup. The subject is the fictional weekly night market whose 18 × 24 in
poster is the `print-design` example; its DNA is that example's. The brief: a series of posts
for the feed, one a week, that people stop on and that reads as the same market every time.

## DNA — the poster's, with the feed's questions

| Ask | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Materials** | paper lanterns; hand-painted price boards on scrap ply; steam; neon tube; plastic stools; cash and a biscuit tin |
| **Motifs** | the price board — one big word, a column of items, prices in a different hand; the plume of steam; the tear-off strip |
| **Palette from source** | lantern orange-red; fluorescent-tube blue; kraft and ply — the poster's two Riso inks on uncoated paper, which the feed can only *draw*, not print |
| **Tempo** | weekly; lettered by hand the same afternoon; loud, crowded, improvised |
| **Vernacular** | "stall", "board", "cash only", "while it lasts", "from 7 till late" |
| **Rules of the world** | prices dominate; nothing is centred by a designer; everything is made cheap and fast and looks it, on purpose |
| **Where it is seen** | a phone, in a feed, at a thumb's distance, for one second, between two other posts; 4:5 (1080 × 1350) is the tallest every scheduler takes, and the feed itself has gone 3:4 (1080 × 1440) since 2025 |

## Directions

1. **The board, cropped.** *Cross-domain grammar* (the price board) at feed size: the word so
   large it runs off the right edge, the board of this week's stalls with their hours where
   the prices go, the running line at the foot. The variable is the week's four stalls.
   *Swap test:* the board grammar is the market's; a gig or a gallery cannot use it unchanged.
   **Survives.**
2. **The plume.** A halftone steam plume in blue filling the post, one line of type.
   *Material.* *Swap test:* a coffee brand, a laundrette, a sauna. **Fails.**
3. **The tear-off.** Each post is the poster's tear-off strip: the perforation, the essentials,
   "tear this off · keep it". *Cross-domain grammar.* *Swap test:* survives — the strip is the
   poster's own. But it is one line of information and the feed needs a reason to stop.
   **Runner-up: the story format** (9:16, a vertical strip) when the series goes to stories.
4. **The lantern.** One warm disc, the word inside, nothing else. *Extreme parameter.*
   *Swap test:* any event with a circle. **Fails.**

## Committed

**Direction 1.** The series is the design: one template, one variable, three weeks.

- **Canvas.** 1080 × 1350, 4:5 — the tallest image every scheduler accepts, so the board has
  room; the feed's own 3:4 (`ig-34`) would give 90 px more and is the variant to cut when the
  client posts by hand. Type ≥ 40 px for anything that must be read in the feed; the word is
  cropped by the right edge on purpose.
- **Faces.** The word in **Big Shoulders Display** at `wght 900` — condensed caps, signage,
  the poster's voice on a face the feed can load; the board in **JetBrains Mono** — the hand
  that writes the prices. Two faces.
- **Inks, drawn.** Orange `#ff5a1f` and blue `#1a3a6b` on paper `#f2e9d6`. The blue copy of
  the word sits 6 px off the orange and multiplies — the poster's misregistration, drawn, not
  filtered. Paper grain once, at 0.06.
- **The variable.** Four stalls with hours, and one "price" line — the thing that is cheap
  this week. Everything else is fixed: position, size, the word, the foot.
- **The foot.** The running caps line: "FRIDAYS · 7 TILL LATE · OLD FERRY LANE · CASH ONLY".
- **Motion.** None. It is a picture in a feed.

One sentence: *a stall's price board, cropped by the phone's edge, with this week's stalls
where the prices go.*
