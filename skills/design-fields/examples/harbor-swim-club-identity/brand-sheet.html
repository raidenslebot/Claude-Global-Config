A life-ring, half under

The mark is a ring with its waterline through the centre — a tow-float sitting half under, the way one does in open water. The wordmark is harbour signage: condensed, medium weight, tracked, and it never raises its voice. Navy of deep water; cream of a rope; orange only where safety uses it.

The mark

Navy on cream. Stroke 4.6% of the width; the waterline half that, two units above centre so it reads as centred.

Reversed: cream on navy. One colour in either; never a third.

Lockups

Horizontal — the wordmark on the ring's centre line. For signs, headers, the membership card.

Reversed.

Stacked — the garment lockup, as on the tee's left chest and the cap.

Reversed — cream ink on the navy blank.

The one variable

Slack water — the waterline at the centre. The mark, everywhere, always.

High water — the waterline at three quarters. Only on materials about a swim at high water; never as decoration.

Never

  • A gradient, a shadow, a rotation, or an outline stroke around the wordmark.
  • The ring without its waterline; the waterline anywhere but the centre or three quarters.
  • Orange for anything but the flags. A third colour of any kind.
